## 3. What each check establishes

1. **Grid & field audit** — every price on the 0.01 tick and every quantity on the instrument lot step; no NaN/negative values. This establishes that the observed data are *compatible* with the configured tick/lot; it does not by itself establish the historical minimum trading increments or fee tiers in force (the exchange metadata endpoint is geo-restricted and was not bypassed). Historical instrument specification and fee schedules must be confirmed before simulated quoting (Stage 3 entry condition, §6).
2. **Ordering audit** — capture-order monotonicity, exchange-time monotonicity of diffs, duplicate levels within a message, update-id monotonicity (HF).
3. **Deterministic replay** (`lpx/book/engine.py`) — messages applied atomically; a snapshot resets the book; sequence gaps, crossed/locked books and unrepresentable levels invalidate it until the next snapshot. **Proven region:** a depth-limited snapshot is treated as proving a side only inside the price range of its best 900 levels (1000-level REST response minus a 100-level margin for buffered diffs the vendor merges in); a touch outside that region is `TOP_UNKNOWN` and excluded. The 900-level margin is a **conservative modeling choice, not an observed guarantee**: the vendor does not mark which snapshot rows came from the REST response; its effect is stress-tested in §4.7.
4. **Engine vs vendor** — the engine's top of book equals Tardis' own derived `quotes` at every published quote (engine correctness, not source independence).
5. **Independent cross-capture** — every HF diff message with contiguous update ids is aligned against the Tardis capture: a complete Tardis capture must reproduce each id-contiguous run as one unbroken, in-order block with identical content.
6. **Independent REST snapshots** — HF's REST depth snapshots taken on two Tardis days are compared level-by-level with the Tardis replay at the best-aligned message. REST responses carry no exchange time, so the instant is not observed: the alignment is *selected by search* over replay messages within −3 s/+1 s of the collector timestamp. Zero discrepancy at the selected message shows that the replay reproduced the exchange's book at some instant in that interval; it does not identify a uniquely timed match. Reported alongside: how many candidates reach the minimum and the mismatch profile across candidates.
7. **Resync comparisons** — at every vendor reconnect, the replayed book just before is compared with the fresh exchange snapshot.
8. **Trade completeness** — vendor trades vs the exchange archive by trade id (missing/extra/field mismatches).
9. **Trade→depth linkage** — every execution changes its maker level, so one of the next two depth events must carry that (side, price); measured on trades inside valid windows.
10. **Silence** — a trade not followed by any depth event within 1 s cuts the window at the trade (threshold stress-tested in §4.7).
11. **Windows** — runs of valid messages end at the last valid message and a new run starts at every resync snapshot (never extended across unobserved time), minus silence cuts (`book` layer) and minus intervals around missing trades, extended to the day boundary at file edges (`joint` layer, required for trade-flow experiments). States are stamped on the exchange clock with the running maximum of message times: a resync snapshot carries only the collector's receive time and a buffered diff applied after it can carry an earlier exchange time (twice in 20 sessions, ≤ 1 ms), so the running maximum never places a state before one it supersedes.

## 5. Canonical instrument: SOLUSDT (evidence)

1. **Validity:** 469.104 of 480 sampled hours (97.73%) are valid on the joint layer, 467.191 h in windows ≥ 600 s; 18 of 20 sessions are ≥ 99% valid. BTC/ETH lose 23–70% of each day to the proven-region rule because a 1000-level snapshot spans only ~15–35 bp (BTC) or ~55–105 bp (ETH) around the touch versus 140–930 bp daily ranges, and §4.3 shows the unproven region genuinely hides resting liquidity. SOL's proven region spans ±4.3–12.3% around the touch.
2. **Economic relevance of the tick:** SOL's one-tick spread is 0.46–1.37 bp of price across sessions (BTC ≈0.0017 bp, ETH ≈0.05 bp). Spread capture, maker fees (Binance regular 10 bp; VIP9 1.1 bp; liquidity-provider rebates −0.4 to −0.8 bp) and adverse selection are of comparable magnitude only for SOL, so the research questions are non-degenerate there.
3. **Tick-constrained regime:** the quoted spread is one tick for ≥ 99.96% of valid time in every session, so quote width is essentially not a decision variable at the touch; the relevant decisions are whether to rest at the touch, queue position, size, skew via one-sided withdrawal, and when to stand down. This is recorded as an observed constraint that the policy design (Stage 4) must respect, not a modeling choice.
4. **Sample design:** 20 first-of-month sessions with a fixed microstructure regime (tick 0.01, lot 0.001, µs exchange timestamps, `depth@100ms`) spanning SOL at 73–219 USDT and daily ranges of 205–929 bp. Days are the natural independent blocks for inference.

## 6. Limitations and unresolved observations

- **Sampling bias:** first-of-month days only (free tier); 20 discontinuous sessions, 6 of them weekend days; no claim about continuous months, holiday effects or intramonth dynamics.
- **100 ms batching:** the public depth stream publishes net level changes every 100 ms. 0.6–2.3% of SOL trades execute at prices not visible in the last published book state; book-based features and counterfactual fills must be timed against this granularity (Stage 1/3 design input).
- **No exchange update ids in the Tardis CSV:** continuity outside the HF-overlap windows rests on the vendor's documented sequence validation and reconnect behaviour, the 100% trade→depth linkage, the silence rule and the resync/REST evidence above — strong but indirect.
- **Day-start gap:** Tardis resubscribes at 00:00 UTC; the book is unanchored for ~1–2 s each day (excluded).
- **Proven-region margin** (900 of 1000 levels) is a conservative modeling choice, not an observed guarantee; results inside it are verified where an independent snapshot exists (§4.3), results outside it are excluded, and coverage under alternative margins is reported (§4.7).
- **Start-of-day and resync snapshots** are vendor-constructed (REST response merged with buffered diffs, not marked row by row); the replay inherits any error in them until the next anchor. Independent REST checks exist only at two instants per overlap day.
- **REST alignment** is selected by search (§3.6); it supports equality of books, not a uniquely timed match.
- **Instrument filters and fees:** data are compatible with tick 0.01 / lot 0.001 on every row, but historical minimum increments and fee tiers are not established by the data. **Stage 3 entry condition:** confirm the historical SOLUSDT filters and fee schedule for 2025-03 → 2026-10 from primary or archived documentation before any simulated quoting.
- **Selection not blind to the holdout:** all 20 sessions' coverage and tick regimes informed the instrument choice; only test outcomes are sealed (`configs/research_v1.toml`).
- **Independent reproduction:** the external reviewer could not execute the pipeline (no `polars`/`pyarrow` there). The earlier statement that numbers had been reproduced by separately written code overstated the record — those review scripts were not retained. Now: `tools/verify_stdlib.py` (standard library only; imports nothing from `lpx`) recomputes trade completeness, a plain-dict book replay against the vendor quotes, and the window hours from raw files, and `tests/test_engine_reference.py` checks the engine against an independently written brute-force reference. Neither is a third-party reproduction.
- **Not evaluated:** SOLUSDT days before 2025-03 (millisecond exchange timestamps, different lot step); OKX (U.S. access unresolved, not bypassed); LOBSTER samples (licence forbids publication of derived results).
