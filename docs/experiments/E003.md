# E003 — Decision-time signals and quoting policies: specification (FROZEN 2026-10-22, before any E003 forecast or policy outcome was computed on market data; revised twice after independent review — record `docs/audits/2026-10-22_e003_spec_review.md`)

**Questions.** RESEARCH_SPEC **H1**: does better out-of-sample short-horizon prediction translate into better
simulated market-making outcomes after costs? RESEARCH_SPEC **H3**: does risk-aware quoting improve downside exposure or
risk-adjusted outcomes relative to more aggressive predictive quoting? And the economic question: at the attainable fee
schedule, is participation rational, or is abstention the better decision?
**Status labels.** Forecast evaluation: *observed* (forecasts and outcomes come from observed data; no own fills).
Policy outcomes: *modeled historical replay* (our orders never existed; fills inferred; no market impact).
**Machine-readable parameters:** `configs/e003_v1.toml`. Execution assumptions (fill models, latency brackets, fees,
filters) are those of E002 (`E002.md`, `E002_assumptions_evidence.md`) unless stated here.

**Prior knowledge (disclosed).** E001 and E002 used all 15 discovery sessions. E001: touch liquidity is adversely
selected on average; trade-direction-conditioned imbalance cells (descriptive, not decision-time). E002: P0/P1 lose
≈ 1–2 bp of notional before fees and ≈ 11–12 bp at VIP0. E003 cannot be blind to these facts. Its protections: every
rule, parameter and decision rule is fixed before any E003 forecast or policy outcome is computed on market data;
forecasts are strictly walk-forward; and each pre-registered decision states the outcome the earlier results already
suggest (section 8), so a result that merely restates E001/E002 is not reported as a discovery.

## 1. Chronological protocol
- Discovery sessions in time order s1…s15 (train 2025-03 → 2025-12, validation 2026-01 → 2026-05). The 5 test sessions
  (2026-06 → 2026-10) stay sealed until the Stage 4 close-out (section 10); the code refuses them.
- **Walk-forward.** For evaluation session s_k (k = 6…15) every fitted quantity — OLS coefficients, clipping bounds, the
  volatility-gate median — comes from the thinned training rows (section 4) of sessions s1…s_{k−1} only.
  **Evaluation sessions: s6…s15 (10 sessions).** s1…s5 are never evaluated.
- **Development checks (s1 only).** Before the run the code may run on s1 to check runtime, invariants, non-degenerate
  feature variance, trade receipt matching and the causality tests on real data. No forecast skill or policy outcome of
  an evaluation session is computed before the run. A defect found this way is fixed and logged in `DECISIONS.md` as a
  pre-run amendment; no parameter is changed for performance.
- Order of work: specification and configuration reviewed and committed → model and runner code → adversarial code review
  → one run on s6…s15 → pre-registered analysis → report. Process note: the engine options of section 6 and a first
  version of the feature code were written while this specification was under review; nothing was run on E003 outcomes.
  The only market-data runs in that period were E002 reruns (runtime timing and the E002 reproduction check).

## 2. Episodes, clocks and the information set
- Episodes as in E002: accepted joint validity windows of at least 600 s. Each starts flat with no orders and ends with
  cancellation and a liquidation-adjusted valuation. The session drawdown path and order-rate counters carry across a
  session's episodes.
- **Decisions** as in the E002 engine: on state S_k at its recorder receipt time t_recv(S_k) plus the market-data delay
  of the latency bracket, and on each fill acknowledgement (using the last received state and its forecast).
- **Information set at S_k (I_k):** the levels of S_k (top 10 per side); earlier valid states of the same episode;
  exchange trades of the same episode that the recorder had *received* by t_recv(S_k) (Tardis `local_us` ≤ t_recv(S_k),
  matched to the Binance Vision record by trade id; a trade without a Tardis receipt time is never used and the count is
  reported); acknowledged inventory and working orders. Every feature is a function of I_k only.
- **Warm-up:** no policy quotes during the first 60 s of an episode (the longest feature window), and warm-up rows enter
  neither fitting nor evaluation. The engine applies the warm-up to decision times (receipt + delay) and eligibility uses
  exchange times, so the first decisions after the warm-up may use a state up to ≈ 0.12 s inside it, whose 60 s window is
  that much shorter (disclosed; negligible).

## 3. Features (fixed)
Windows are (t_exch(S_k) − w, t_exch(S_k)] in exchange time, restricted to I_k and to the episode. Prices in bp are
relative to mid(S_k), so they do not drift with the price level (the relative tick is 0.46–1.20 bp across the sample).

| Name | Definition |
|---|---|
| `imb1` | (q^b_1 − q^a_1) / (q^b_1 + q^a_1) |
| `imb5` | (Σ_{i≤5} q^b_i − Σ_{i≤5} q^a_i) / (Σ_{i≤5} q^b_i + Σ_{i≤5} q^a_i) |
| `ofi_1s`, `ofi_10s` | Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov best-level order-flow imbalance summed over the state transitions in the window, divided by the mean touch depth (q^b_1 + q^a_1)/2 of the window's states |
| `flow_1s`, `flow_10s` | signed trade quantity / total trade quantity of the window's received trades (0 without trades) |
| `ret_10s` | log mid change from the last state at or before t_exch(S_k) − 10 s to S_k, in bp |
| `spread` | spread of S_k in ticks − 1 |
| `rv_60s` (volatility gate only, not a regressor) | √Σ (Δ log mid in bp)² over the window's state transitions |

**Target.** y_h = 10⁴ · log(mid(t + h) / mid(S_k)) in bp, where t = t_recv(S_k) + 30 ms (the regional decision time)
and mid(τ) is the mid of the last valid state with exchange time ≤ τ. Rows with t + h beyond the episode end are excluded.
This selects on the future path, because episodes end when the touch leaves the proven region (as in E001/E002);
disclosed for R²_OS. Horizons h ∈ {1, 5, 30} s; **primary h = 5 s**.

## 4. Forecast models (walk-forward; no tuning)
- **M0** zero forecast. **M1** OLS of y_5 on an intercept and `imb1`. **M2** OLS of y_5 on an intercept and the 8
  regressors of section 3. Policies use the h = 5 s fits.
- **Training rows:** every 5th eligible (post-warm-up) state row of each training session, in time order, with a defined
  target. **Sessions are weighted equally** (weight 1 / rows of the session), so busy days do not dominate.
- Regressors are clipped at the 0.5% / 99.5% quantiles of the training rows; the bounds are reused at prediction. The
  target is not clipped. No regularisation, selection, search or non-linear model (gradient boosting and other ML are not
  part of E003: earned, not assumed).
- In the policies, a forecast f in bp is converted to ticks with the mid of S_k: f_t = f · mid_ticks(S_k) / 10⁴.

## 5. Policies (one working order per side; 1 SOL; nothing tuned)
Common to all: post-only orders at the visible touch or none; a side is posted only if a full fill keeps the
acknowledged inventory within ±10 SOL; a working order at the wanted price is kept (queue retained); warm-up and order
pacing as in sections 2 and 6. s = spread in ticks; f_t = forecast in ticks; q = acknowledged inventory (SOL);
c = maker fee in ticks = maker rate × mid in ticks (VIP0 ≈ 8–22 ticks at SOL prices of about 80–220 USDT).

| Policy | Bid at the touch iff | Ask at the touch iff | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NT** | never | never | no-trade baseline |
| **P0** | always | always | fixed (E002 P0) |
| **P1** | q < 3 | q > −3 | inventory-aware (E002 P1: withdraw the side that adds to \|q\| ≥ 3 SOL) |
| **P3[M]**, M ∈ {M1, M2} | s/2 + f_t > 0 | s/2 − f_t > 0 | alpha-aware, fee-unaware |
| **P3[−M2]** (placebo) | s/2 − f_t > 0 | s/2 + f_t > 0 | same withdrawal frequency as P3[M2], wrong direction |
| **P3F[M2]** | s/2 + f_t − c > 0 | s/2 − f_t − c > 0 | alpha- and fee-aware (aggressive predictive quoter) |
| **P4** | P3F's condition with f_t − κq, and q < 3, and gate open | P3F's condition with f_t − κq, and q > −3, and gate open | combined risk-aware |

- P4 = P3F[M2] + inventory skew (κ = 0.25 tick per SOL: one tick of reservation shift per 4 SOL) + P1's withdrawal +
  a volatility gate (no quotes while `rv_60s` > 3 × the median `rv_60s` of the training rows). κ, 3 SOL and 3 × are set
  a priori, not fitted.
- The rule "s/2 ± f_t > 0" is a heuristic. It uses the expected mid move from the decision, not the selection
  conditional on being filled; P3F's and P4's fee gates therefore overstate a rational quoter's participation.
- P3 with M0 is P0. P3[M2] vs P3[−M2] holds participation nearly fixed and reverses the forecast's direction: the H1
  contrast. P4 vs P3F[M2] differs only in the risk components: the H3 contrast.
- Fee-unaware policies (NT, P0, P1, P3[·]) are simulated once, fee-free; fees are exact overlays. P3F and P4 depend on
  the maker fee and are simulated once per fee schedule (VIP0, VIP9, LP tier 4).
- RESEARCH_SPEC's P2 (adaptive spread/size) is not a separate policy. With a one-tick spread, widening means backing off
  the touch and losing queue priority, and its risk role is covered by P1's withdrawal and P4's gates. Size stays fixed at
  1 SOL; adaptive size is deferred (logged in `DECISIONS.md`).

## 6. Execution, fees, rate limits
- **Fill models** conservative, intermediate, optimistic × **latency** Tokyo-hosted, regional, distant = 9 scenarios.
  **Primary scenario: intermediate × regional.** The naive model is not used.
- **Order pacing (replaces E002's hard daily-cap cliff).** In E002, P0 reached the 200,000/day cap in some optimistic
  sessions and then stopped quoting for the rest of the day, at a time that differed by fill model. (E002's VIP0
  conclusions are unchanged on the 12 sessions where no primary-model scenario reached the cap: exploratory addendum,
  `results/e002/addenda/cap_free_subset.json`.)
  In E003 every policy draws new orders from a per-side token bucket of 1.15 orders/s with 20 tokens of burst, carried
  across the session's episodes. Cancels are free. A wanted order without a token is not sent (the stale order has
  already been cancelled). By construction at most 2 × (1.15 × 86,400 + 20) = 198,760 orders/day and
  2 × (20 + 1.15 × 10) = 63 per 10 s can be sent, so the exchange limits (200,000/day, 100/10 s) cannot bind; the runner
  asserts zero exchange-limit blocks and re-checks both limits from the recorded send times.
  **Pacing does bind:** E002's P0 wanted up to ≈ 1.24 orders per side per second averaged over its busiest sessions, and
  more in bursts. Pacing therefore acts as a fast-market throttle on every policy, and its incidence differs by policy
  and fill model (fills consume tokens for replacements). Reported per policy, scenario and session: the share of valid
  time during which a side was wanted but unquoted for lack of a token (*paced time share*), and the number of distinct
  paced spells (a spell starts when a wanted order is refused and ends at the next send on that side or when the side is
  no longer wanted). The H3 interpretation discusses its overlap with P4's volatility gate.
- **Fees.** VIP0 (10 / 10 bp) is the attainable default and the primary schedule. Hypothetical: VIP9 (1.1 / 2.3 bp) and
  LP tier 4 (−0.8 / 2.3 bp).
- **Engine changes** (E002 re-review minors and E003 needs): a post-only rejection is not re-sent on the same stale
  state when a fill notice arrives first; the first daily-cap block time, quote presence, paced spells and time, and a
  5-minute wealth grid are recorded. All are switched off under E002's configuration, and E002's tables must be reproduced
  exactly at the new revision before the E003 run.

## 7. Outcomes
Per (session, policy, scenario, fee schedule), liquidation-adjusted at every episode end:
- **Economic:** net P&L (USDT); **net P&L per valid hour**; net and gross bp of traded notional (undefined without fills,
  never imputed); gross per hour; maker fees; liquidation cost; fill edge; inventory revaluation.
- **Participation:** quote presence (share of valid time with a resting order on the bid, on the ask, on both); fills;
  traded notional per valid hour (turnover); fill rate; orders sent; orders per fill; post-only rejections; paced time
  share and paced spells; exchange-limit blocks (must be 0); **gate activity** of P3[·], P3F and P4 (share of decisions
  on which the forecast/fee gate removed a side the base rule wanted); P4's volatility-gate closed share (by period:
  bid-ask bounce may make bp-volatility read higher at higher relative ticks).
- **Risk:** maximum and time-averaged |inventory|; maximum drawdown of the session wealth path (per fee schedule).
  5-minute P&L increments: on an exchange-clock grid from each episode start, liquidation-adjusted wealth is sampled at
  the first state at or after each grid time and at the episode end (the last increment is shorter and includes the
  liquidation). From all increments of a session (zeros included): standard deviation; **expected shortfall ES = −(mean
  of the ⌈0.05 n⌉ smallest increments)**, a positive loss; the worst increment. Participation-normalised: ES and
  time-averaged |inventory| per 1,000 USDT of hourly turnover.
- **Own fills:** edge vs the mid before the fill; markouts at 1, 5, 30 s (E002 definitions).
- **Forecasts** (M1, M2; every evaluation session; eligible rows; regional decision clock): R²_OS = 1 − Σ(y − f)² / Σ y²
  (benchmark: zero forecast); IC = Pearson correlation of f and y; sign hit rate on rows with f ≠ 0 and y ≠ 0;
  calibration by forecast decile; share of rows with |f_t| ≥ 0.5 tick. h = 1 and 30 s: same regressors refitted on
  that target (descriptive).

## 8. Pre-registered analysis (evaluation sessions s6…s15)
Unit: session (10). Mean of session values; 10,000-draw session-bootstrap percentile intervals (seed fixed); paired
differences on the same sessions; the count of sessions with a positive difference is reported next to every interval
(ten sessions are few and percentile intervals can under-cover). A contrast enters a decision only if it is defined in at
least 8 of the 10 sessions. VIP0 and the primary scenario unless stated.

**Primary family (3 decisions; 95% intervals; Bonferroni 98.33% intervals reported, and a decision that changes under
them is labelled *not robust to multiplicity*).**

1. **H1-F — forecast skill.** M2 has out-of-sample skill if its session-mean R²_OS at h = 5 s has an interval above 0.
   *Ex-ante expectation from E001:* likely to hold (imbalance relates to short-horizon moves).
2. **H1-T — translation.** Contrast D = gross bp of traded notional (liquidation-adjusted, before fees) of P3[M2] minus
   that of P3[−M2]. Fees are a constant rate on notional and cancel to first order, so D measures fill quality, not fee
   burden. Margin δ = 0.5 bp (a quarter tick per policy at the median relative tick; about a third of E002's gross loss
   per notional).
   - **H1 supported** (skill without material translation): H1-F holds and D's upper bound < δ.
   - **H1 refuted** (skill translates materially): H1-F holds and D's lower bound > δ.
   - **Inconclusive** otherwise. **Not testable** if H1-F fails, or — *rule inactive* — if the gate activity of P3[M2] or
     of P3[−M2] (session mean) is below 5%: then both policies are nearly P0, D ≈ 0 by construction, and a small D would
     say nothing about translation.
   Also reported for H1, not as decisions: D on net per hour; P3[M2] − P0 (does the forecast beat fixed quoting?); the
   ranking check — M2 − M1 in R²_OS against P3[M2] − P3[M1] in gross bp; whether P3[M2] is profitable after fees.
   *Ex-ante expectation from E002:* P3[M2] loses after VIP0 fees whatever D is, so after-cost unprofitability alone is
   never reported as support for H1. *Ex-ante expectation for D:* no strong prior; E001 suggests imbalance carries some
   direction, so D > 0 is plausible, but its size relative to δ is unknown.
3. **H3-risk — risk components (hypothetical LP tier 4, both policies simulated under it).** P4 − P3F[M2]:
   - downside: the ES difference and the maximum-drawdown difference both have intervals below 0;
   - non-inferiority: the net-per-hour difference has a lower bound above −δ_h, with δ_h = 0.10 × |mean net per hour of
     P3F[M2]| (a margin of 10% of the comparator's mean outcome, defined before the run; δ_h is a point estimate whose own
     sampling uncertainty is not propagated — disclosed).
   - **H3 supported** if both hold. **H3 refuted** if the net-per-hour difference has an upper bound below −δ_h or the
     ES difference has an interval above 0. **Inconclusive** otherwise.
   - If P4's turnover is below 50% of P3F[M2]'s, the result carries the label *risk reduction partly mechanical*, and the
     participation-normalised metrics are reported next to it.
   - The same contrast under VIP0 and VIP9 is descriptive. Under VIP0 both fee-aware policies are expected to abstain;
     if either trades less than 1% of P3[M2]'s notional, it is labelled *not testable: abstention*.
   - *Ex-ante expectation:* none strong. Risk components should cut inventory and drawdown; their effect on net is unknown.

**Participation decision (economic, VIP0, primary scenario).** A trading policy is preferred to no-trade only if its
net-per-hour interval, Bonferroni-adjusted over the 7 trading policies (P0, P1, P3[M1], P3[M2], P3[−M2], P3F[M2], P4;
99.29%), lies above 0. If none qualifies, the report states that abstention is the economically rational decision at the
attainable fee schedule for these policies on these sessions, gives each policy's participation and loss, and shows how
both change under the hypothetical schedules. *Ex-ante expectation from E002:* abstention.

**Descriptive (no decisions):** all 9 scenarios; hypothetical fee schedules; train-late (s6–s10) vs validation
(s11–s15) sessions; horizons 1 and 30 s; calibration; E003 P0 vs E002 P0 on the same sessions (effect of pacing and
warm-up); pacing incidence; net per hour / standard deviation of 5-minute increments; trades without a receipt time.

## 9. Acceptance criteria before the E003 run
1. E002 re-review test gaps closed: runner plumbing (`to_states`, `to_trades`, guards), analysis refusals, post-only
   rejection latency, 10 s window expiry and carry across episodes, P1, an independent order-count check replacing the
   tautological one, the held-rejection fix, `net_per_hour` defined without valid time.
2. E002 reproduction at the new revision with E002's configuration: every frozen row of `e002_gross_by_session.csv`
   reproduced exactly, for all 15 sessions.
3. Toy and synthetic tests for: pacing (budget, carry, exchange limits unreachable, paced spells and time); cap-bind
   time; quote presence; 5-minute grid and ES; every policy rule including P3F's and P4's gates and the placebo; warm-up;
   **feature causality** (perturbing states, trades or trade receipt times after a state leaves its features unchanged;
   trades received after t_recv(S_k) and data outside the episode are never used); **forecast attachment** (each state
   receives the forecast computed for exactly its (t_exch, t_recv), asserted at run time); **walk-forward** (a model for
   s_k uses sessions before s_k only); session-weighted OLS and R²_OS on synthetic data with known coefficients; the
   analysis decision rules on synthetic tables, including each H1/H3 label.
4. This specification and `configs/e003_v1.toml` committed before any E003 forecast or policy outcome is computed on
   market data, after an independent review with no unresolved critical or major finding.
5. Adversarial code review of the E003 code: no unresolved critical or major finding.
6. Runner: clean tree; provenance before and after; input hashes; refuses test sessions, an uncommitted specification
   and an existing output directory; per-episode accounting identity; fills inside episodes; no crossing fills outside
   the optimistic model; zero exchange-limit blocks; no order during warm-up.
7. Development check on s1 passed (runtime, invariants, receipt matching: 0 of 1,658,774 trades without a receipt time,
   feature ranges). It found that float running sums let `flow_1s` exceed 1 over 1.6 M trades; fixed before the freeze
   with exact integer lot arithmetic and a test.

## 10. Close-out on the test sessions (Stage 4 only)
The 5 test sessions are run once with the frozen code and configuration through a separately guarded entry point,
added and logged in `DECISIONS.md` before the close-out. Models, clipping bounds and the gate median are fitted on all
15 discovery sessions (training rows as in section 4) and are not refitted on test data. The same tables are computed.
Five sessions are too few for interval decisions, so confirmation is a point-estimate sign check: a primary decision
that held on discovery is *confirmed* if its contrast has the same sign on the test sessions and, for H1-T, the same side
of δ; for H3, the ES, maximum-drawdown and net-per-hour differences each have the discovery sign and the net-per-hour
difference stays above −δ_h. Decisions that were inconclusive or not testable on discovery are only reported.

## 11. Known limitations
All E002 limitations apply (no market impact; modeled queue position; invisible iceberg and intra-batch flows; latency
brackets are calibrations; VIP0 fees for 2025-03 → 2025-10 inferred). In addition: linear forecasts fitted on 5–14
sessions; the future-path selection of target rows; heuristic fee and forecast gates that ignore fill-conditional
selection; pacing as a throttle shared by all policies; policies are fixed rules, not optimised, so negative results
speak to these rules, not to every possible policy; ten evaluation sessions give low power; first-of-month sampling.
